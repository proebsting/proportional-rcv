STV (Single Transferable Vote) Demonstration

This demo simulates a Single Transferable Vote election on a simplified one-dimensional political spectrum (positions 1–100). Voters are modeled as two normal distributions along that spectrum; each voter ranks all candidates in order of proximity to their own position. STV then fills the available seats through successive rounds of quota-based election and elimination, transferring surplus or exhausted votes until all seats are filled. Adjust the distributions and candidate positions below and watch the results update in real time.

A group of voters centered around a position on the spectrum, modeled as a normal distribution. Adjust the mean (center position), std dev (spread), and total number of voters. You can also drag the blue handle on the histogram — left/right changes the mean, up/down changes the spread.

Election Settings

The number of seats to be filled in this election. The Droop quota — the minimum votes a candidate needs to win a seat — is calculated automatically as ⌊votes / (seats + 1)⌋ + 1. More seats generally leads to more proportional representation.

Voter Distribution

Combined histogram of all voters across the spectrum. Candidate positions are shown as vertical lines — green with a checkmark if elected, red with an ✗ if eliminated. The dashed purple line marks the median voter and the dashed orange line marks the mean. Drag the blue (D1) or red (D2) handle to adjust that distribution interactively.

STV Results

Round-by-round results of the STV count. The ballot progression table shows how each ballot group's votes are used or transferred across rounds. Each subsequent round shows either a candidate reaching the Droop quota (surplus votes transferred fractionally) or the lowest candidate being eliminated (their votes redistributed to next preferences).
